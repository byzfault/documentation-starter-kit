## CTO · Folk Digital (UK) and Shero Commerce (US)

Two chief technology officer roles at digital agencies, one either side of the Atlantic. Both were owner-level positions: I sat in the room where the commercial and technical direction got decided, and I carried the consequences.

**What I owned:**

- Engineering headcount, hiring and mentoring across distributed teams of up to 20
- Platform architecture for client builds and internal products
- DevOps, CI/CD, environment strategy and release process
- Delivery standards, estimation, code review culture and definition of done
- Technical due diligence on new business, including which work to turn down

**Distributed by default.** The teams spanned multiple countries and time zones well before it was standard practice. That means async handover, written decisions, and process that survives people being offline for eight hours at a stretch.

<Callout type="info">
**On the owner-level part.** Being CTO at an agency is not only an engineering job. You are pricing risk, defending estimates to clients who want them lower, and deciding when the honest answer is that the plan will not work. I have made that call in front of paying clients more than once.
</Callout>

## How I Run Delivery

**Scope.** Get the shape of the work agreed in writing before anyone estimates it. Most overruns I have seen were scope disputes wearing an engineering costume.

**Decisions.** Write them down, with the reasoning and the discarded options. A decision nobody can reconstruct in six months gets relitigated in six months.

**Cadence.** Short feedback loops, working software early, and a release process boring enough that nobody dreads Fridays.

**Escalation.** Bad news travels fast or it compounds. I would rather tell a client in week two that the date is at risk than in week nine that it has gone.

**Handover.** Every engagement ends. The measure of a good one is whether the team can carry it without me.

## Team Leadership

- Hiring, onboarding and progression for engineers at all levels
- Mentoring leads into their first management roles
- Code review and quality standards that raise the floor without stalling delivery
- Performance conversations, including the difficult ones
- Keeping engineers close enough to the commercial context to make sensible trade-offs on their own

**What I look for in a team:** people who flag problems early, write things down, and can explain their work to somebody outside engineering. Those three habits are worth more than raw output.
