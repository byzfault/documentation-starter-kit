## CTO · Folk Digital (UK), 2014 to 2016

Boutique brand-led agency for luxury and fashion clients. I held the highest technical position: solution architecture, platform strategy and engineering delivery across concurrent enterprise accounts.

**What I owned:**

- A team of 12 to 15 mixed-skill developers, organised in pods
- The full people cycle: CV screening, recruiters, multi-stage interviews with a working task, hiring, weekly 1:1s, mentoring, work allocation and the hard calls
- Pre-sales and scoping: sizing, integration feasibility and resourcing for sales. I pitched to Wedgwood and Pepe Jeans and co-owned the client-signed scoping document
- 4 to 10 builds a year, the largest around £300k with enterprise licensing, with me as technical project manager on the bigger ones
- Client steering sessions, proposals, architecture documentation and integration specifications
- Cloud infrastructure, DevOps, security and performance

**A seat in the leadership meeting.** I sat on the leadership and operations meeting as the technical voice for the business.

<Callout type="info">
**Scope of the CTO title.** I owned delivery and team decisions outright. I influenced the larger strategic architecture and partnership calls and did not always win them. The agency was small and those calls were contested.
</Callout>

## How I Run Delivery

**Scope.** Get the shape of the work agreed in writing before anyone estimates it.

**Decisions.** Write them down, with the reasoning and the discarded options.

**Cadence.** Short feedback loops and a release process boring enough that nobody dreads Fridays.

**Escalation.** Bad news travels fast or it compounds. I would rather tell a client in week two that the date is at risk than in week nine that it has gone.

**Handover.** Every engagement ends. The measure of a good one is whether the team can carry the work without me.

## Team Leadership

- Weekly 1:1s, and deep ones. I adapted how I communicated to each person
- Hiring with a working task in the interview
- Mentoring weaker developers until they were useful
- Hard conversations. I have had to let a developer go, and I did not enjoy it

**What I look for in a team:** people who flag problems early, write things down, and can explain their work to somebody outside engineering.
