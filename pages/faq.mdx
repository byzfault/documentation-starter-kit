### How do you approach an AI adoption engagement?

This is the approach I am using now with the MDES design team at Mastercard.

1. **Reconcile the standards.** Turn inconsistent guideline documents into instructions an agent can apply, and decide the ambiguous cases
2. **Control false positives.** Define the quality bar and test on real release-cycle work
3. **Embed it.** Fit the tool into the team's workflow so it stays in use

The agents are in scoping. They are not live yet.

---

### How do you handle subject matter experts who are too busy?

Do the homework first. Read the code, run the product, understand the basics. Then ask specific questions: not "explain this" but "I think X works like Y, where am I wrong". A focused 30 minutes beats a rambling two hours. Show work in progress so people have something to react to.

## Process Questions

### What does a typical first month look like?

<Steps>

### Weeks 1 to 2
- Understand the product, the team and the goals
- Map stakeholders and decision rights
- Inventory where AI tools are already in use
- Agree priorities in writing

### Weeks 2 to 4
- Choose a small set of use cases with real value
- Write the standards the tools apply
- Test on real work and record the false positives

### Ongoing
- Decisions documented as they are made
- Risk raised early, not at the deadline
- The team able to carry the work without me

</Steps>

## Questions I Ask You

Before we work together I want to understand:

1. **What is the adoption or delivery problem?** Late, unclear, under-resourced, or all three
2. **Who owns the decisions?** And who thinks they do
3. **What is the current state?** Greenfield, legacy, or halfway through
4. **What does success look like?** Measures, goals, timeline
5. **How does the team work?** Remote, async, meeting culture, reporting lines

Most delivery failures are organisational before they are technical. I would rather find that out in the first conversation than the third month.
