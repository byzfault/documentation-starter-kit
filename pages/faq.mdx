# FAQ

The questions hiring managers and clients actually ask. The two awkward ones are first.

## The awkward ones

### You say you build with AI. Do you write the code yourself?

No, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

I read and review code, and I have done for twenty years: developer, lead developer, CTO at two agencies, senior solutions architect. That is what lets me review an architecture, audit a codebase, or tell you why an integration will fall over against a real catalogue.

What I do not do is sit and hand-write production code. I ship with Claude Code CLI. I own the problem, the scope, the architecture, the review and the deployment. The tool writes the implementation. The [portfolio](/portfolio) is what that produces, and the source is public so you can judge it.

If you need someone typing into a keyboard for eight hours a day, hire that person. If you need someone who can go into a customer, work out what to build, and have a working version running that week, that is the offer.

### Your last two contracts were documentation-led. Are you an engineer or a writer?

Engineer, for twenty years, and the last two contracts put me inside the systems I wanted to be in.

Mastercard put me in a live global tokenisation platform, working with product owners and engineering on the thing itself. Starknet put me at protocol level on an Ethereum Layer 2 alongside cryptographers. Both were delivery engagements with content as the artefact: scope negotiated against a live product, priorities set from operational data, competing stakeholders, a fixed pipeline and a date.

What I am doing now is going back to owning the build, in the payments and blockchain space those contracts took me into.

## The role

### Have you been a Forward Deployed Engineer before?

Not under that title. FDE is what I am targeting, and it is the closest available name for the work I have been doing since 2010.

The pattern is the same one: go to the customer, run the discovery, find out that the stated problem is not the problem, scope what is actually needed, design it, build the first working version, get it deployed, hand it over. I have done that as a lead developer, as CTO at two agencies, as a senior solutions architect running technical discovery alongside sales at Klevu, and as an independent consultant. The only new part is that the build step is now AI-assisted, which is why the first version arrives in days.

- I ship working software with agentic tooling rather than producing a deck about it

In payments, tokenisation, blockchain and Layer 2, enterprise commerce, APIs and developer tooling: effective immediately. Adjacent technology in a familiar domain: one to two weeks. Genuinely new domain: two to four weeks of ramp-up while I build context.

I learn fast where the material is hard. I will not pretend to expertise I do not have.

## Technical

### Will you be hands-on?

Yes. I read code, review architecture, and debug a broken pipeline rather than filing a ticket about it. I build and ship tools with Claude Code. I am not the right hire for a role that needs a developer writing features by hand full time.

### Can you build the infrastructure, or only direct it?

Build it. Docs-as-code pipelines, CI/CD, deployment automation, custom sites in React and Next.js, linting and validation, search integration, analytics. At Starknet I owned the documentation platform and its deployment pipeline as well as the content. This site and both portfolio tools are mine end to end, built with Claude Code.

### How do you handle subject matter experts who are too busy?

Senior engineers are always busy and I do not expect them to do my work.

1. **Do the homework first.** Read the code, run the product, understand the basics
2. **Ask specific questions.** Not "explain this" but "I think X works like Y, where am I wrong"
3. **Use their time well.** A focused 30 minutes beats a rambling two hours
4. **Show work in progress.** Give people something to react to
5. **Earn the access.** Once you clearly understand the system, availability stops being a problem

## Working together

### What does a new engagement look like?

<Steps>

### Assessment, weeks 1 to 2
Read the system, meet the stakeholders, find where the real problem is and write it down

### Planning, weeks 2 to 3
Scope agreed, sequence agreed, first deliverable defined and small enough to land

### Execution, week 3 onward
Working software early, short feedback loops, decisions documented as they are made

### Handover, throughout
Written as it goes, so the team can carry it when the engagement ends

</Steps>

### Contract or permanent?

Both. Contract suits contained scope at a senior level, and I am open to permanent for the right role. Not interested in full-time on-site.

### How do you measure success?

The thing is in production and people are using it, the team can maintain it without me, and the decisions still make sense a year later. If a programme ships predictably and the architecture is still defensible after twelve months, the job was done.

### Where are you based, and what about time zones?

South of the UK, remote by default. UK and European hours preferred, US East Coast workable. I have run distributed teams across time zones since well before it was normal. Occasional travel is fine.
