Starknet was moving from testnet to mainnet with a new programming language, a proof system most developers had never touched, and an ecosystem of teams waiting to build on it. I owned the developer-facing documentation and portal through that transition.

## The Situation

The protocol was still being finalised while developers were already building against it. Cairo was changing, and so was its toolchain. Every question a developer had went to a core engineer, and core engineers were the scarcest resource in the organisation.

## Scope

- **The product loop for the docs.** I prioritised against a constantly shifting protocol roadmap with core engineers and cryptographers, collected ecosystem feedback, and used analytics to decide what to build and fix: heatmaps, session capture, usability tests and A/B tests
- **Content for a wide range of readers.** Scalability trade-offs, system architecture, STARK proofs and smart-contract usage, for audiences from ZK researchers to dApp developers
- **Cairo.** I managed the rapid redesign of the Cairo language documentation as the language and toolchain changed
- **Process and open source.** Community pull requests, code review and merging, contribution guidelines, versioning and multi-language, docs-as-code and CI/CD for the docs site, and automation experiments
- **Community and marketing.** Community support and technical marketing with the DeFi and dApp teams building on Starknet. Meetups and conferences. I managed multiple external agencies to scope and budget

## Decisions That Shaped It

**Go to the source.** I worked directly with cryptographers and protocol engineers. Holding the conversation without everything pre-digested meant better questions.

**Ship against the protocol's timeline.** Mainnet had a date. I published against what was decided and revised as things settled, which meant accepting that some pages would change twice.

**Let analytics set the work.** Heatmaps and session capture showed where readers got stuck. That decided what to fix first.

<Callout type="info">
**Working with research teams.** Cryptography and protocol engineers are precise people working on hard problems. I do the homework first and arrive with a specific claim for them to correct.
</Callout>

## Stakeholders

Core protocol engineers, cryptography researchers, the DeFi and dApp teams building on the network, external agencies, and marketing.

## Why This Matters for Payments and Stablecoin Work

Layer 2 infrastructure and the economics of moving value on-chain are the ground stablecoin infrastructure is built on. I worked inside that technology, under launch pressure. Combined with the Mastercard tokenisation work, that covers card networks and on-chain settlement. I am domain-literate in both.
